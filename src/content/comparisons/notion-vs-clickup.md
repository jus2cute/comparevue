## The Culture Fit

Notion attracts design-forward, documentation-heavy teams. It's the tool where you start writing a meeting note and realize you've built a project tracker, an onboarding guide, and a company handbook — all interconnected with relational databases. Content teams, HR departments, and internal documentation thrive here.

ClickUp attracts process-driven, productivity-focused teams. If your day involves task assignments, deadlines, sprints, Gantt charts, and resource planning, ClickUp is built for that. It also includes decent docs, so you don't have to juggle two apps.

## Who Should Use Notion?

Teams that live in documents. Knowledge management, wikis, SOPs, meeting notes, and research databases — if your workflow revolves around written content and flexible organization, Notion is the best tool for the job. The database feature (with views like table, board, calendar, and gallery) makes Notion uniquely powerful for structured documentation that goes beyond simple pages.

Great for content teams, HR, internal documentation, and anyone building a company wiki that needs to be both beautiful and functional.

## Who Should Use ClickUp?

Teams that manage projects and track progress. If your workflow centers on tasks with deadlines, dependencies, and resource planning, ClickUp's project management features are far stronger than Notion's basic task views. The built-in Gantt charts, time tracking, and workload views give you project visibility that Notion simply doesn't have.

Best for lean teams that want to replace multiple tools (project management, docs, whiteboards, chat) with one platform — saving money and reducing context-switching.
