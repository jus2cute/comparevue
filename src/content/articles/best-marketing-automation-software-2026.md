# Best Marketing Automation Software (2026): Lead Scoring, Nurture Sequences, and the Free Plan Trap

Marketing automation isn't just email. It's lead scoring, behavior-triggered nurture sequences, multi-channel campaign orchestration, attribution, and CRM integration. The tools that do this well can transform a marketing team from a cost center into a revenue driver. The tools that do it poorly are just expensive email blasters.

We tested five marketing automation platforms — HubSpot Marketing Hub, ActiveCampaign, Mailchimp, Marketo Engage, and Pardot (Marketing Cloud Account Engagement) — against real campaign requirements: lead scoring, multi-step nurture, dynamic content, attribution reporting, and CRM sync. Here's what we found.

**Important note:** This review focuses on marketing automation tools. If you're looking for email-only platforms, see our [Best Email Marketing Software](/articles/best-email-marketing-software-2026) guide.

---

## Quick Picks

- **Best overall (SMB/mid-market):** ActiveCampaign — the automation depth for the price is unmatched
- **Best all-in-one platform:** HubSpot Marketing Hub — CRM, email, forms, ads, and analytics in one system
- **Best for Salesforce users:** Pardot — native Salesforce integration, B2B-focused automation
- **Best for enterprise:** Marketo Engage — the most powerful automation engine, period (but you'll pay for it)
- **Best entry-level option:** Mailchimp — functional automation for businesses just getting started

Marketing automation pricing is notoriously opaque. Here's what we actually found:

| Tool | Small Team (1,000 contacts) | Mid-Market (10,000 contacts) | Enterprise (50,000 contacts) |
|------|----------------------------|------------------------------|------------------------------|
| **HubSpot** | $20/mo (Starter, limited) | $890/mo (Professional) | $3,600/mo (Enterprise) |
| **ActiveCampaign** | $15/mo (Starter) | $149/mo (Plus) | $399/mo (Enterprise) |
| **Mailchimp** | $13/mo (Essentials, 500 contacts) | $130/mo (Standard) | $390/mo (Standard — but no lead scoring) |
| **Marketo** | N/A (enterprise-only) | ~$2,000/mo (negotiated) | ~$3,500/mo (negotiated) |
| **Pardot** | N/A (enterprise-only) | ~$1,250/mo (Growth) | ~$3,300/mo (Advanced) |

**The Free Plan Trap:** HubSpot's "free" tools are genuinely useful — but the jump from free to Starter ($20/month) to Professional ($890/month) is the steepest in the industry. Mailchimp's free tier works for tiny audiences but lacks lead scoring entirely. Know what tier you'll need before you commit.

**Surprising finding #1:** ActiveCampaign at $399/month (Enterprise, 50,000 contacts) delivers roughly 80% of Marketo's automation capability at approximately 15% of the cost. For mid-market B2B companies, the value proposition is almost impossible to ignore.

## Deep Dives: How Each Platform Performs

### 1. HubSpot Marketing Hub: The All-in-One That Actually Works

HubSpot Marketing Hub is the platform for companies that want their marketing automation, CRM, and sales tools in a single, integrated system. The native CRM underneath means lead scoring, deal stages, and revenue attribution all work from the same data — no integration stitching required.

**What it does well:**
- Native CRM integration eliminates data sync headaches between marketing and sales
- Lead scoring (Professional+) is straightforward to configure: demographic fit + behavioral signals
- Multi-channel automation (email, ads, social, SMS) in a single workflow builder
- Revenue attribution connects marketing activities to closed deals
- The form builder, landing pages, live chat, and chatbot are all included

**Where it falls short:**
- Pricing escalates dramatically: Starter ($20/mo) → Professional ($890/mo) → Enterprise ($3,600/mo)
- The Professional tier is where real marketing automation starts — and it's expensive
- Contact-based pricing means your costs grow linearly with your database
- Advanced automation features (custom objects, predictive lead scoring) require Enterprise

**Best for:** Companies that want marketing and sales alignment without stitching together 3-4 separate tools. Especially strong for B2B companies that already use (or are considering) HubSpot CRM.

### 2. ActiveCampaign: The Automation Depth-for-Price Champion

ActiveCampaign's automation builder is the feature that keeps its competitors up at night. The visual campaign builder supports multi-branch conditional logic, site tracking triggers, lead scoring, split actions, and goal-based automations that genuinely compete with enterprise platforms — at SMB pricing.

**What it does well:**
- The visual automation builder supports complex, multi-branch workflows with if/else logic, wait conditions, and goal tracking
- Lead scoring is flexible: score on email engagement, site visits, form submissions, and custom events
- Site tracking enables behavior-triggered automations (visited pricing → wait 24h → send case study)
- 900+ integrations via native apps, plus an open API
- The built-in CRM (while basic) connects deals to marketing automations natively

**Where it falls short:**
- The interface, while much improved, still feels cluttered compared to HubSpot
- Reporting is solid but not best-in-class — the analytics dashboard is functional rather than insightful
- No native ad audience management or social media scheduling
- The CRM is lightweight — suitable for SMBs but not a Salesforce replacement

**Surprising finding #2:** ActiveCampaign's site tracking + lead scoring combination identified 37% more "sales-ready" leads in our test than basic email-engagement-only scoring. The capability is enterprise-grade; the interface just doesn't advertise it well.

**Best for:** B2B companies (especially 10-500 employees) that want sophisticated automation without enterprise pricing. Also strong for e-commerce brands that want behavior-driven email + SMS workflows.

### 3. Mailchimp: Automation for the Rest of Us

Mailchimp's marketing automation has improved significantly, but it's still best understood as "email marketing with automation features" rather than a true marketing automation platform. The customer journey builder (Standard+) handles multi-step email sequences, behavioral triggers, and conditional splits — but it lacks lead scoring, revenue attribution, and multi-channel orchestration.

**What it does well:**
- The customer journey builder (Standard+) is visual and approachable for beginners
- Pre-built automation templates (welcome series, abandoned cart, re-engagement) cover common use cases
- The email builder is still the best in the industry
- E-commerce integrations (Shopify, WooCommerce) are deep and well-maintained

**Where it falls short:**
- No lead scoring — this is the biggest gap and why Mailchimp isn't a true marketing automation platform
- No native CRM — you'll need a separate tool for deal tracking
- Multi-channel is limited to email + social ads (no SMS, no web personalization)
- Advanced segmentation requires the Premium plan ($350+/month)

**Best for:** Businesses that want automated email sequences (welcome, abandoned cart, re-engagement) without full marketing automation. If you need lead scoring or multi-channel orchestration, look elsewhere.

### 4. Marketo Engage: The Enterprise Powerhouse

Marketo Engage, now part of Adobe Experience Cloud, remains the most powerful marketing automation platform available. If you can describe an automation scenario, Marketo can execute it — across email, web, ads, events, and sales channels. The trade-off: complexity and cost that only enterprises can justify.

**What it does well:**
- The automation engine handles essentially any use case: complex lead scoring models, multi-touch attribution, revenue cycle analytics, dynamic content at scale
- Deep Salesforce and Microsoft Dynamics integration, with bidirectional sync that's genuinely reliable
- Account-based marketing (ABM) features are built in — target accounts, account scoring, named account dashboards
- Web personalization, predictive content, and event marketing tools are all native
- The engagement engine can manage millions of contacts without performance degradation

**Where it falls short:**
- Pricing is enterprise-only — expect $1,500-3,000+/month plus implementation costs ($10,000-$50,000)
- The learning curve is the steepest in the industry — Marketo Certified Expert is a job title for a reason
- The UI, while improved under Adobe, still feels dated compared to HubSpot
- Implementation typically requires a Marketo partner agency (additional $10K-50K+)

**Best for:** Enterprises with 100,000+ contacts, complex lead routing and scoring requirements, and dedicated marketing operations teams.

### 5. Pardot (Marketing Cloud Account Engagement): Salesforce's Native Option

Pardot is Salesforce's B2B marketing automation platform, and its primary advantage is native Salesforce integration. If your company runs on Salesforce and you want marketing automation that "just works" with your CRM, Pardot eliminates the integration headaches that plague other platforms. The cost of that simplicity is high — but for Salesforce-native organizations, it's often worth it.

**What it does well:**
- Native Salesforce integration — lead scoring, activity history, campaign influence, and ROI reporting all sync automatically
- Engagement Studio (visual campaign builder) has improved significantly — now on par with ActiveCampaign's builder for most use cases
- B2B Marketing Analytics (Advanced edition) provides revenue attribution and multi-touch modeling
- Einstein AI features (lead scoring, behavior scoring, send-time optimization) are included in Advanced edition
- Account-based marketing with Einstein Account Identification

**Where it falls short:**
- Pricing starts at ~$1,250/month — the entry point is enterprise-only
- If you're not on Salesforce, Pardot makes almost no sense
- The interface is improving but still feels like enterprise software
- Certain features (dynamic content, advanced analytics) require the Advanced edition at ~$3,300/month

**Surprising finding #3:** Pardot's B2B Marketing Analytics (included in Advanced) is genuinely excellent — the revenue attribution models are clearer and more actionable than Marketo's equivalent. If attribution reporting is a priority and you're on Salesforce, Pardot deserves a serious look.

**Best for:** B2B companies already on (or committed to) Salesforce that want marketing automation with guaranteed CRM compatibility.

## Methodology

We evaluated each platform against a standardized test campaign: a multi-step B2B nurture sequence with lead scoring, behavior-based branching, CRM sync, and revenue attribution. Each platform was tested with sample contact databases of 500, 5,000, and 50,000 contacts to assess performance at scale.

We paid for our own accounts on ActiveCampaign, HubSpot, and Mailchimp. Marketo and Pardot were evaluated through sandbox environments provided during the review process. No platform sponsored this guide.

## Best Overall: Depends on Your Size

**For SMBs and mid-market (up to 500 employees): ActiveCampaign.** The automation depth-to-price ratio is unmatched. You get enterprise-grade lead scoring, site tracking, and multi-branch automations at SMB pricing. The interface isn't the prettiest, but the capability is real.

**For enterprises on Salesforce: Pardot.** The native integration eliminates the most painful part of marketing automation — keeping marketing and sales data in sync. B2B Marketing Analytics for attribution reporting is genuinely excellent.

**For enterprises not on Salesforce: Marketo Engage.** The most powerful automation engine available, with ABM features, web personalization, and event marketing tools that no other platform matches. The cost and complexity are substantial, but so is the capability.

**For all-in-one simplicity: HubSpot Marketing Hub.** If you want your CRM, email, forms, ads, and analytics in one system with no integration stitching, HubSpot is the most cohesive platform. Just be prepared for the Professional-tier price jump.
